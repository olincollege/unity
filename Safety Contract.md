# 2. Safety Contract

UNITY is a shared research cluster. UMass runs it, not Olin, and we share it with thousands of researchers at other schools. It has a password on it and that's about it. **It is not a secure system.** Olin is only allowed to put open data on it, and our whole arrangement with UMass depends on us holding to that.

Read this and sign at the bottom.

---

## 1\. Private data

**Nothing that identifies a person goes on this cluster.** That includes:

- Names, emails, student ID numbers, addresses, phone numbers  
- Grades, transcripts, course rosters, anything about how a student is doing. This is FERPA data and Olin is legally bound to protect it.  
- Survey responses, interview recordings, interview transcripts  
- Photos or video of people  
- Health or medical information of any kind  
- Payment or financial information  
- Scraped data that still has usernames, handles or user IDs in it  
- Anything someone shared with you under a confidentiality agreement

**There is no exception for any of these:**

- "It's anonymized." De-identified data can often be re-identified.  
- "It's only going to be there for an hour."  
- "It's zipped up" or "it's encrypted."  
- "It's just a small sample" or "it's just test data."  
- "It's my own information about myself."  
- "My professor said it was fine." Check with us anyway.

**If you're not sure whether your data counts, ask before you upload it.** That question is never annoying and we would much rather answer it. Deleting the file afterward does not undo having put it on a shared system.

**If private data ends up on UNITY, tell us immediately**, whether you put it there or not. Reporting it fast is the right move and won't be held against you. Sitting on it will.

Breaking this rule gets your account closed. Serious cases go to the Honor Board, and they can cost every Olin user their access to the cluster.

---

## 2\. Your account

Your account is yours. Don't share your login, SSH keys or tokens, and don't let anyone else run work under your name.

Don't try to get around security. No poking at other people's files, no working around permissions, no hunting for holes. If you find one by accident, tell us. You won't be in trouble for finding it.

Use UNITY for Olin coursework, research and independent projects. No commercial work, no crypto mining, nothing illegal.

## 3\. Sharing the cluster

Don't run programs on the login node. It's for editing files, moving data and submitting jobs. Everyone connects through it, so heavy work there slows things down for everybody.

Close sessions when you walk away and cancel jobs you don't need. A session you forgot about is still holding a machine.

Ask for about as much time and memory as you actually need. Run something small first, then run `seff` afterward to see what the job used. Asking for way too much puts you further back in the queue and blocks other people at the same time.

Clean up. Delete files you're done with, find out which directories get wiped automatically, and take your data with you when the project ends or you leave Olin.

Tell us when something is broken, and report it to UNITY support too. Don't try to fix shared infrastructure yourself.

If you don't know how to do something, ask.

## 4\. What we can and can't promise

UNITY is provided as is. It goes down, jobs get killed, filesystems get purged. **Back up anything you can't afford to lose.** Olin can't recover your work and isn't responsible for lost computation or missed deadlines.

If a law, license or data use agreement applies to data you bring onto the cluster, following it is on you.
